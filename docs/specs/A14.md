# A14 RCM Operations Control Tower

- **Phase:** MVP; **release:** Foundation baseline, then Wave 1 (expanded); **milestone:** M4 (baseline), M11 (expanded)
- **Build status:** Baseline built (M4): nine KPIs as SQL with an as-of date (docs/metrics.md). Expanded scope built in M11, shadow mode only (`krisnnai/agents/a14_tower/`, `krisnnai tower`, `krisnnai a14 sweep|replay`): cross-agent decision inbox, cash forecast with intervals and a measured accuracy, backlog, four anomaly signals, and a daily escalation review. No UI yet (FastAPI cannot load on the dev PC); the tower dict is JSON-ready for one.

## 4. Deterministic rules and payer/client policy inputs

Plain code, no model (rule 6). `compute_tower(engine, tenant, as_of)` is read-only and point in time. It returns KPIs, a cash forecast, the open work across A5, A6, A7 and A8
(the **decision inbox**), backlog, signals and the number of pending approvals.

- **Open work.** The latest card per claim from each agent's decision records known by `as_of`, minus anything resolved: A5 and A7 close when the payer answers the claim; A6 closes when
  the payer answers or the claim is re-filed; A8 closes on a later paying remit. Ranked by priority (A7 and A6 weight dollars by urgency, A8 uses expected recovery, A5 the charge at
  risk; the scales are not calibrated against each other, a sign-off item).
- **Cash forecast.** For an open claim of age a with the payer's smoothed share q of mature, unrejected claims that ever get paid and its days-to-pay curve F (from paid mature claims),
  the chance it is paid in week k is q * (F(a + 7k) - F(a + 7k - 7)) / (1 - q * F(a)). A claim already older than anything the payer has paid is forecast near zero. A payer with
  fewer than 5 paid mature claims gets a flat 60-day ramp (flagged). Amount = A15 expected payer payment. Interval = 80% (1.28 sd) from independent pay / do-not-pay variances.
- **Signals** (all thresholds are placeholders for sign-off): `backlog_growth` (open items up by 25% and at least 5 in a week), `payer_slowdown` (median days to pay of the last 30 days
  at least 7 days slower than the 90 days before, at least 5 paid claims each), `rejection_spike` (rejection share of claims submitted in the last 14 days at least 5 points above the
  60 days before, at least 10 claims), `deadline_critical` (any open item due within 7 days or overdue; never muted).

## 6. Action options and prohibited actions

**Built action:** `a14_escalation_review` (owner: operations leader), at most one per day, proposed when any signal fires; it carries the signals, the backlog, the 28-day forecast
and the top of the inbox as a standard card. It changes no resource, assigns no work and writes nothing back. **Not built, needs data the repo does not have:** productivity
and staffing forecasts (task / action logs and staff capacity), load-balancing recommendations (staff data), a fitted forecasting model.

Forecasts cash, backlog and staffing; detects queue anomalies, payer slowdowns and process bottlenecks; recommends load balancing and escalation. Baseline scope in the first 30 days: KPI and cash baseline only. Prohibited for every agent: any change to CPT, ICD-10 or modifiers, any write-off or refund, and any submission to a payer without a named human approval; submission only through the partner clearinghouse adapter; PM/EHR write-back limited to tasks and notes until each action type is approved under strategy Decision 8.

## 13. Shadow-mode evaluation dataset and acceptance criteria

Dataset and acceptance criteria are fixed before shadow results are reviewed: TBD at spec review.

**Cash forecast accuracy (the A14 KPI).** `backtest` forecasts on past weekly dates and compares the next 28 days with what was actually paid on the same open claims. On the synthetic
open-AR set (`generate(n_open=200)`, 400 claims over 200 days, fixed payer delays, 10% stuck, 12.5% returned), eight weekly forecasts gave a WAPE of 6% and the actual landed inside the
80% range in 6 of 8. Thinner history is looser (`n_open=100`: 21% WAPE; `n_open=40`: 30%), because payers with too little history fall back to the flat ramp. Two flaws were found and fixed
while building it: (1) building the days-to-pay curve from all paid claims made payers look fast, so it now uses mature claims only; (2) the chance a claim ever pays counted claims the
277CA had returned, but the forecast excludes those, so it now uses unrejected claims only. A first synthetic set also picked its open claims because they were unpaid, which made any
backtest unfair; the generator now lets payment timing decide what is open. These numbers show the method works on data with a known answer; they say nothing yet about a real practice.
